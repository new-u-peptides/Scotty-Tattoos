Why here

A small island with a lot of tattoo shops.

Most of them will tattoo you anything. I'll tattoo you one thing, properly.

Malta has no shortage of studios, and plenty of them do good work across a wide range of styles. I went the other way. Sixteen years in, I tattoo geometric and ornamental work exclusively — sacred geometry, mandalas, dotwork, blackwork and the large ornamental pieces that take a body part rather than a corner of one.

That narrowness is the point. When you spend every week of the year drawing the same language, you stop guessing. You know how a mandala has to sit on a shoulder so it still reads when the arm drops. You know how much negative space a dotwork gradient needs to survive a decade of Maltese sun. You know which lines to thicken now so the piece is still sharp when you're fifty.

It also means I'm the wrong artist for a lot of ideas, and I'll tell you that early rather than take the booking. If you want a colour portrait or a traditional flash piece, Malta has excellent people for it — they're just not me.

If you can't get to the island, the same work travels — see travel and guest spots.

Finding us

Birkirkara, dead centre of the island.

The studio is Massa Tattoo Social Club, 11 Triq Il-Knisja Il-Qadima, Birkirkara. Central Malta, which in practice means about fifteen minutes from Valletta, ten from Sliema and St Julian's, and reachable on most of the island's main bus routes.

There's street parking in the surrounding roads. Come as you are — eat beforehand, bring headphones, bring a friend if you want one. Sessions are private and unhurried; there's no queue behind you and nobody rushing the chair.

Everything runs by appointment. That's not gatekeeping — it's because the drawing happens before you arrive, and a custom design can't be produced while you wait.

Travelling in

Getting tattooed while you're here.

A good share of my chair goes to people who fly in. It works well — as long as you plan it around the two things Malta does to healing skin. The full checklist is in the guide to getting a tattoo abroad.

Send the idea before you book the flight

Custom work needs design time — usually one to three weeks between the consultation and the session, longer for a sleeve. The earlier your enquiry lands, the better the drawing you sit down to.

Book it for the end of the trip

A fresh tattoo can't go in the sea and can't sit in direct sun. Tattoo on day one of a beach holiday and you'll spend the week covered up or wrecking the work. Tattoo on the last day and you fly home healing properly.

Plan for the heat

Maltese summer is hard on healing skin — sweat, salt and sun all work against fresh ink. Between May and September I'll walk you through a tighter aftercare routine than I'd give someone healing through a northern European winter.

Build it over more than one visit if it's big

Sleeves and backs don't finish in a week. Several clients run their projects across annual trips — we map the whole design at the start, then take a chunk each visit. It keeps the piece coherent instead of stitched together.

How it runs

From enquiry to healed.

Enquiry

You send the idea, the placement, a rough size and any references. No commitment, and no deposit at this stage.

Consultation

At the Birkirkara studio, or a video call if you're abroad. We work out what the piece actually is, where it sits and how many sessions it needs.

Design

I draw it for your body and nobody else's. You see it before the appointment and we adjust until it's right.

The session

Stencil mapped to anatomy, then the work. Private, unhurried, with breaks whenever you need them.

Healing

You leave with a clear aftercare plan and a way to reach me if anything looks off. Touch-ups on my work are part of the deal.

Questions

Getting tattooed in Malta.

How much does a tattoo cost in Malta?

Studio rates across Malta vary widely. My own work is quoted per project rather than per hour once I've seen the idea, the placement and the size, because a custom design involves drawing time before any needle work starts. Small single-session pieces are quoted as a flat price; sleeves, backs and legs are quoted as a multi-session project with a session rate. Send an enquiry with your idea and rough placement and you'll get a real number rather than a guess. There's a fuller breakdown of what drives tattoo prices in Malta in the journal.

Where is the studio in Malta?

Massa Tattoo Social Club, 11 Triq Il-Knisja Il-Qadima, Birkirkara — central Malta, roughly fifteen minutes from Valletta and ten from Sliema. Birkirkara sits on most of the island's main bus routes, and there is street parking in the surrounding roads. The studio works by appointment, so there is no walk-in queue.

Can I get a tattoo in Malta while I'm on holiday?

Yes, and a lot of my clients do exactly that — but book before you fly. Custom work needs design time, so the further ahead you send your idea, the better the piece will be. The other thing to plan for is healing: a fresh tattoo cannot go in the sea or sit in direct sun, so schedule it for the end of the trip rather than day one.

Do you take walk-ins?

No. Every piece is drawn for the person wearing it, which means the design work happens before the appointment. Everything runs by enquiry and consultation, either at the Birkirkara studio or on a video call if you're still abroad.

What language do you work in?

English throughout — enquiry, consultation and the session itself. Maltese and Italian are both widely spoken at the studio too.

What styles of tattoo do you do in Malta?

Geometric and ornamental work only — sacred geometry, mandalas, dotwork, blackwork and large-scale ornamental pieces. I don't work in realism, colour portraits or traditional flash. Specialising in one language is what keeps the standard where it needs to be, and if your idea sits outside it I'll say so and point you somewhere better.
